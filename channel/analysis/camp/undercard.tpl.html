The finding

The main event is a walkover

Every starting job in the NFL is a slot on a depth chart, and every day since March that chart has been photographed. Which means you can count, precisely, how often a job actually changes hands.

Sort those moves by where they sit on the depth chart and the shape is the opposite of the coverage.

The competition doesn't thin out below the starter. It intensifies.

Three and a half times as much movement at the third string as at the first. The job being fought over in August is not the starting job. It is the fifty-third one.

The calendar

And the fight was last spring

Four hundred and eighty-nine starter changes in March. Thirteen in July. The peaks land on free agency, the franchise tag window, and the fifty-three man cutdown — never on camp.

What this actually measures. These are ESPN's depth charts, not any team's — teams don't publish real ones until late August. So this is the informed outside view changing its mind, which is a different thing from a coach changing his. Only two seasons carry the timestamped data, and there is no July 2025 baseline, which means it cannot yet separate rosters are settled from the chart goes quiet in summer. The instrument is demonstrably awake — 79 moves in cutdown week, 30 to 60 every week in season — but awake in November is not proof of awake in July.
