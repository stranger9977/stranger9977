# The Money

You asked for a channel that grants financial freedom. Here's the honest arithmetic,
including the part that argues against the obvious plan.

**The headline: ad revenue is the worst-paying monetization route at every scale, and it
is not your path to replacing a senior data scientist salary. Your path is selling
expertise to a small, well-targeted audience, with the channel as lead generation.**

Confidence tags as in `00-strategy.md`. `[HIGH]` is verified this session, `[LOW]` means
the number traces to SEO content farms and should not be a plan input.

---

## 1. The thresholds

`[HIGH — consistent across multiple independent sources]`

| Tier | Subs | Watch hours (12mo) | OR Shorts views (90d) | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fan funding | **500** | **3,000** | 3M | Memberships, Super Thanks, Shopping affiliate. **No ad share.** |
| Full YPP | **1,000** | **4,000** | 10M | Ad revenue, Premium share |

Plus two-step verification, a linked AdSense account, no active strikes, an eligible
country. The 500 tier also needs 3 public uploads in the prior 90 days. Review takes
about 1–4 weeks after you cross.

**Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000 watch hours.** It's a separate, parallel
path — and a trap. 10M Shorts views in 90 days is 111,000 views/day sustained, and at
Shorts RPM of roughly $0.01–$0.10 that's a channel's worth of work for a few hundred
dollars. Ignore the Shorts path to monetization.

**Which threshold binds?** Do the arithmetic: 4,000 watch hours is 240,000 watch-minutes.
At a 4-minute average view duration that's about **60,000 lifetime views**. Meanwhile
1,000 subscribers at a 1% conversion rate needs about **100,000 views**. So **subscriber
count is usually the binding constraint**, not watch hours. Optimize for subscriber
conversion, which means: end every video with a reason to come back, not a plea to
subscribe.

## 2. The base rates, stated honestly

`[HIGH]` This is the part most creator advice omits, and you should see it before
building a plan on top of it.

- **Bärtl (2018), *Convergence*** — a proper academic study, random-sampled: the **top 3%
  of channels receive 85–90% of all views.** This is a genuine power law with a fat tail.
  The median outcome is near zero by construction.
- **Linktree 2022 Creator Report** (n=9,576): **46% of *full-time* creators earn under
  $1,000/year.** **Only 12% of full-time creators earn over $50,000/year.** Note the
  direction of the selection bias — these are people motivated enough to set up a
  link-in-bio, so if anything it *overstates* the median.
- `[MEDIUM]` Roughly 90% of channels have under 1,000 subs; about 619,000 channels
  worldwide have 100k+, which is around 0.5% of all channels.

That 12% number is the single most important figure here: **among people who already call
themselves full-time creators — already conditioned on surviving selection — about one in
eight clears $50k.**

This is not a reason to skip it. It's a reason to build the version that doesn't depend
on winning the lottery, which is what the rest of this document is about.

## 3. Why AdSense can't get you there

The formula is `views = ($ target / RPM) × 1,000`.

`[LOW on the RPM level, HIGH on the structure]` At a sports RPM of $3–6:

| Monthly target | Monthly views needed at $5 RPM |
|---|---|
| $1,000 | 200,000 |
| $5,000 | 1,000,000 |
| $10,000 | 2,000,000 |
| $20,000 | 4,000,000 |

To replace a senior data scientist salary — call it $180–250k gross — you'd need roughly
**3–7 million views a month, every month.** That's a top-fraction-of-one-percent channel,
sustained, in a niche whose RPM is mid-table.

And note the seasonality trap: `[MEDIUM-HIGH]` Q4 RPM runs 25–35% above Q1. Every income
screenshot you'll ever see is a December month annualized. Discount accordingly.

**Conclusion: plan for AdSense to be 10–15% of revenue, not the revenue.**

## 4. What actually pays on a sports channel

`[MEDIUM]` Reported revenue mixes converge on AdSense being a minority stream. For sports
specifically the AdSense share should be *lower* than the cross-niche 30–40% benchmark,
because sports has weak ad CPMs but unusually strong sponsor demand.

A modeled blend at ~100k subs and ~400k views/month — **this is inference stacking
reported unit economics, not measured data**:

| Stream | Monthly | Share |
|---|---|---|
| AdSense | $1,200–2,400 | 10–15% |
| Sponsorships (2–4 integrations) | $4,000–12,000 | 40–50% |
| Affiliate | $1,000–6,000 | 10–25% |
| Memberships | $1,750–5,000 | 12–18% |
| Newsletter | $500–1,500 | ~5% |
| Merch (profit) | $600–2,500 | ~5% |
| **Total** | **~$9,000–28,000** | |

### Sponsorships — the anchor number

`[MEDIUM — from the one sports-betting-specific rate card found]` **Mid-tier YouTube
sports channels at 50–200k subs command $2,000–6,000 per dedicated integration.**

Back-solve that: a 100k-sub channel doing 30–60k views per video is charging an effective
**$35–100 CPM** on betting deals. That's *finance-vertical* pricing, well above the
generic sports/entertainment $15–30. Sports audiences are cheap to reach with ads and
expensive to reach with sponsorships, and you want to be on the second side of that.

**Who's actually buying** `[MEDIUM]`, and this is genuinely actionable:

- **Underdog Fantasy: 84 YouTube channels, 3,047 sponsored videos.** By far the most
  aggressive buyer of creator inventory in the space.
- **DraftKings: 5 tracked channels.** They spend on broadcast and team deals, not
  mid-tail creators. Do not build a plan around a DraftKings deal.
- **Prediction markets are the 2026 volume buyers.** Polymarket sponsored 101+ YouTube
  videos and Kalshi 140+ in a single trailing 90-day window, across hundreds of creators.
  Both are actively soliciting sports creators. No published CPMs found.

⚠️ **Read section 7 before pursuing any of these.**

### The newsletter — the most underrated line

`[HIGH — this is the best-sourced revenue finding in the research]`

beehiiv's *State of Paid Newsletters 2026*:

- Platform median free-to-paid conversion: **0.62%**
- **Sports has the highest median conversion of any vertical, at 1.93%** — roughly 3x the
  platform median. The typical sports newsletter converts better than the typical finance
  newsletter.
- Average paid price: **$10/month or $100/year**, flat since 2024.

And the proof case, which maps almost exactly onto your situation:

**Extra Points (Matt Brown)** — college sports as an *industry*, not as fandom. beehiiv's
own case study is titled **"$200K newsletter with 2,000 true fans."** Later reporting has
it at ~16,000 total subscribers with 3,000+ paying, with revenue diversified into
FOIA-driven data sales to athletic directors. Acquired by D1.ticker.

**~2,000–3,000 paying subscribers is a $100–200k/year business.** That is a completely
different order of difficulty than 3 million monthly views, and it's reachable from a
100k-subscriber channel — or arguably from a much smaller one.

Compare directly: **Huddle Up (Joe Pompliano)**, sports business, hit six figures in under
a year.

**Start the list on day one.** Not at Level 3 — day one. It is the only asset YouTube
can't take from you, and for a sports analytics channel it is probably the single highest
expected-value revenue line you have.

### Affiliate

`[MEDIUM]` Sportsbook CPA runs **$120–350 per first-time depositor, median around $200** —
the highest per-unit economic in the stack. RevShare is 25–40% of gross gaming revenue,
but the deduction stack (bonuses, taxes, fees) guts it at creator volumes; CPA beats
RevShare unless you're sending genuinely high-LTV bettors.

For contrast, ticket-resale affiliate is a rounding error: TickPick pays 4–5%, SeatGeek
about 1% on NFL. One sportsbook conversion at $200 equals roughly eleven TickPick orders.

⚠️ **Sportsbook affiliate has state-level licensing requirements that were not researched.
Do not model this as income until you've checked. And read section 7.**

### Memberships

`[MEDIUM]` Sports behaves like entertainment: **0.5–1% of subscribers convert.** YouTube
takes 30%, so a $4.99 member nets $3.49. Multi-tier setups raise revenue per member
30–50% over single-tier.

At 100k subs that's roughly 500–1,500 members, or **$1,750–5,000/month**. Real, but not
transformative, and it arrives late.

## 5. The path this research actually points to

Here's the finding that should reshape the plan. The audience required for $10k/month
spans **three orders of magnitude** depending on the business model:

| Model | Audience required for ~$10k/mo |
|---|---|
| AdSense | 500,000–1,000,000 views/month |
| Sponsorships | 250,000–400,000 views/month |
| $200 product | 10,000–20,000 engaged email subscribers |
| **$5k/mo B2B service** | **~1,000–3,000 targeted followers. Sometimes a few hundred.** |

Two retainer clients is a materially easier problem than a million monthly views, and
it's reachable in months rather than years.

**And you are unusually well-positioned for the bottom row specifically.** You are not a
generalist creator hoping to sell a course about making videos. You are a Senior Data
Scientist who builds NFL and NCAA simulation and pricing models, placed twice in the Big
Data Bowl against 300–400 person fields, and can produce analysis that essentially nobody
else in the creator space can.

The people who need that — teams, agencies, media companies, betting operators, fantasy
platforms, private analytics shops — are a small, identifiable, well-funded audience, and
they are *watching the kind of content you make* precisely because it demonstrates the
capability.

**So the honest structure of the business is:**

```
Against the Book (channel)      →  demonstrates the capability, builds the name
        ↓
Newsletter (email list)         →  owns the audience, converts at 1.93% in sports
        ↓
Paid research tier / data       →  the Extra Points model, $100–200k at 2–3k subs
        ↓
Consulting & retainers          →  the fastest route to real money, needs ~no audience
```

Ad revenue funds the hobby. The newsletter funds the transition. The expertise funds the
life.

The timeline table, honestly `[MEDIUM]`:

| Milestone | Realistic for a serious, consistent operator |
|---|---|
| 0 → YPP | 6–12 months of weekly-ish posting. Many never get there. |
| 0 → $1,000/mo total | 12–24 months |
| 0 → $10,000/mo from AdSense alone | 2–4 years, high-RPM niche only. Most never. |
| **0 → $10,000/mo via services/products off a small audience** | **6–18 months** |

Only that last row is a plan rather than a hope.

## 6. How to read any case study you encounter

`[HIGH]` Because you will encounter a lot of them, and nearly all are worthless:

1. **There is no denominator.** Case studies are sampled on the outcome. Nobody publishes
   the identical playbook that produced nothing.
2. **The case study is usually the product.** Most "how I did it" content is made by
   people whose income comes from teaching how to do it. Their $10k/mo is often *from
   telling you about the $10k/mo.* Check what they sell before believing the timeline.
3. **Almost no fast case study is a true zero start.** Graham Stephan was already a
   successful realtor. Ali Abdaal was a Cambridge doctor whose credential *was* the
   content. Shaan Puri already had an audience. (Note: you also have a credential that
   *is* the content — that's a real advantage, and it's the same one they had.)
4. **Screenshots aren't evidence.** Weight journalist-verified earnings (the Business
   Insider series, where reporters saw actual dashboards) and platform-public data
   (Patreon counts) far above self-reports.
5. **"Hit $10k once" ≠ "earns $10k/mo."** Ask for twelve consecutive months.
6. **Strategies have short half-lives**, and a playbook gets sold as a course precisely
   when its edge has decayed.

## 7. The conflict-of-interest problem, stated plainly

This is not legal or employment advice, and it isn't researched — it's flagged because
it's the kind of thing that's expensive to discover late.

**You work at Penn Entertainment, which operates a sportsbook.** Nearly every
high-paying monetization route identified above — sportsbook sponsorships, sportsbook
affiliate CPA, prediction-market deals — is in or adjacent to your employer's industry,
using skills adjacent to your day job, under your own name.

Things worth resolving before the channel is big enough to matter:

- Your employment agreement's outside-activity, moonlighting, and IP-assignment clauses
- Whether analysis built on public data (nflfastR is public; your employer's models are
  not) is cleanly separable — keep a bright line and never let it blur
- Disclosure obligations to your employer
- State-level licensing for sportsbook affiliate arrangements
- `[MEDIUM]` YouTube's November 2025 gambling policy: sports betting content was carved
  out as an exception and remains viewable, but **verbal references to gambling sites not
  approved by Google are prohibited.** A channel that name-drops the wrong sportsbook is
  exposed.

**Recommendation: keep betting content, betting sponsors, and betting affiliate entirely
out of season one.** You lose the highest-CPM sponsorship category and you keep your job,
your credential, and your optionality. The newsletter and consulting paths don't require
any of it, and they're the better business anyway.

Revisit once you've read your agreement and, ideally, talked to an employment lawyer for
an hour. That's the cheapest insurance available here.

## 8. What to do in the first 90 days

1. **Ship videos.** Nothing below matters without a library.
2. **Start the email list immediately.** Link in every description, one line at the end of
   every article. Sports converts at 1.93% — the best of any vertical.
3. **Do not chase sponsors.** Below ~10k subs most offers are affiliate-only anyway.
4. **Do not enable memberships yet.** They convert at 0.5–1%; you need the audience first.
5. **Read your employment agreement.**
6. **Notice who emails you.** The first consulting inquiry will arrive far earlier than
   the first meaningful AdSense cheque, and it's worth more.
