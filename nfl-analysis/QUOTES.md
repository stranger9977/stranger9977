## Deliberately excluded

- **"Three things can happen when you pass and two of them are bad."** Attribution is
  genuinely disputed. Earliest print is Lee McLaughlin (Washington & Lee), Nov 1961;
  also credited to Darrell Royal (who used it in his 1963 book, then later credited
  Hayes), Woody Hayes, Robert Neyland and Duffy Daugherty. Too muddy to put a name on.
- **"Three yards and a cloud of dust" as a Hayes coinage.** He did not coin it; the
  phrase predates him and he adopted it at a 1959 clinic. Quote #14 uses his actual words.
- **"Power wins football games."** (Parcells) and **Sean Payton on running games**:
  could not be verified on a retrievable primary source.
- **Two widely circulated Woody Hayes "book" quotes** about establishing a sound running
  game: not present on the cited source when checked.
- **Mike Tomlin "wins by attrition"**: no primary source found; appears to be a drifted
  paraphrase.
- Aggregator quote sites (BrainyQuote, AZQuotes, football-quotes.com) were not used.
  One of them publishes explicitly *imaginary* coach quotes.

## Useful counterpoint (not in the video)

Nick Saban, Mar 26, 2021, to Louisiana HS coaches: "Whoever rushes the ball the most,
for the most yardage is going to win the game. **You're not going to win anything now
doing that.**" ([saturdaydownsouth.com](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/news/college-football/nick-saban-no-longer-believes-in-elite-defenses-but-alabamas-unit-could-be-just-that-in-2021/))
Four years later he was back to "you got to be able to establish the run" on GameDay,
which is its own comment on how durable the idea is.
