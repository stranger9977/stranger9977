TheGrind

Establish the run and the pass opens up. It is the oldest article of faith in football. We tested it five ways.

Mostly myth · one grain of truth
"You wear them down. Establish the run, impose your will, and by the fourth quarter, by December, they break." Every run-first coach, some version of it
1stThe next play

Run, run, then throw

The everyday version is about sequence: pound it on first and second down, and the third-down throw comes open.

So we lined up every third-down pass by how the offense got there, holding the third-down distance fixed. That control matters: how you reach third down decides the yardage you face.

3rdThe wear-down

Nobody breaks

Forget sequence, says the coach. It is about attrition. Keep running and the front seven cracks late.

Run efficiency is flat across all four quarters. And a team's fourth-quarter running is completely unrelated to how much it pounded the ball in the first half.

+0.00early carries vs 4th-quarter running

Fourteen hundred team-games. Whatever the grind is doing, it is not softening anybody up for the fourth quarter.

4thThe reverse

The pass sets up the run

Here is the part that turns the whole idea inside out.

Runs work when the box is light. And a light box is something the passing game earns: the more a team throws, the fewer defenders it sees stacked in the box.

Run vs light box (6 or fewer)39%
Run vs even box (7)37%
Run vs stacked box (8 or more)35%
+0.51correlation: throwing more, lighter boxes

That is the correlation between how much a team throws and how light a box its runs face. You do not run to set up the pass. You pass to open up the run.

4th & 1The grain of truth

December football

Here the run-first coach finally lands a punch, and the numbers back him.

Passing is a big edge in a dome or in September. Drop the temperature and that edge melts. Below 25 degrees it falls by three-quarters, to the point where it is no longer distinguishable from zero. Heavy wind does the same.

Above 70°F+0.15
46 to 55°F+0.12
26 to 35°F+0.09
25°F and below+0.04

The passing edge, in expected points per play. It shrinks toward nothing in the cold. But it never flips. Throwing stays the marginally better bet even on a frozen field, and teams barely change their mix to match.

The verdict

Running does not set up the pass. Not on the next snap, not in the fourth quarter, not through play-action. Defenses do not wear down. If anything the causality runs backwards: throwing is what thins the box and makes the run work.

The one thing the grinders get right is the weather. When it is freezing or blowing, the gap between throwing and running nearly closes. It just never opens the other way.
