01The two sins

How a timeout dies

A timeout is worth roughly a tenth of a point when a two-minute drill actually arrives. Small, but coaches throw them away in two distinct ways, and they are not the same coaches.

Sin one

Burned early

An offensive timeout called before the last four minutes of a half, almost always to escape a play-clock jam. The bailout. League average has fallen from 0.98 per game in 2000 to 0.62 today.

Sin two

Left on the table

Walking off a one-score loss with a timeout still unused. You needed the clock and you never spent it. League average: 15% of one-score losses.

Sort every head coach since 2000 on both and the extremes are stark.

Where this year's coaches stand

Zoom in on the coaches who will be on your screen this fall and something jumps out: managing the clock early and managing it late are two different skills, and most coaches only have one.

McVay and Shanahan burn timeouts early at some of the highest rates in the league, then almost never leave one unused with a game on the line. Stefanski and Harbaugh are the mirror image: tidy before the two-minute warning, then repeatedly walking off one-score losses with one still in hand.

03The twist

The clock-management joke

No coach gets mocked for clock management more than Andy Reid. It has been a running punchline for two decades.

4of 61 one-score losses

Times Reid walked off a one-score loss with a timeout unused. That is 7%, against a league rate of 15%, and the best mark of any coach with a large sample.

He burns a few extra early, which is where the reputation comes from and where the broadcast catches him. But when the game is genuinely on the line, he is the most reliable timeout manager of his generation. The joke has it backwards.

The verdict

Timeouts get wasted in public. Every burn is on camera, every unused one sits on the scoreboard as the clock runs out. The numbers just tell you who does it and how often.

The caveat worth stating: a banked timeout is worth about a tenth of a point, not the half-point the broadcast implies. These are small edges. But they are also free, and the same names keep appearing at both extremes for a reason.

Data: nflverse / nflfastR play-by-play and games, 2000 to 2025, 14,030 team-games, playoffs included.
