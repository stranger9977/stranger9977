Andy Reid · the readable genius

Every broadcast says the great play-callers keep a defense guessing. The numbers say the best ones are the easiest to read in football.

Verdict: backwards
First, the definition

What "predictable" actually means

Picture yourself as the defense. Before the snap you know three things: the down, the distance, the score. You have to call it. Run or pass.

Take one spot every team faces. Second and long. A passing down. You call pass. How often are you right?

2nd and 8 or more to go. You call pass.

Reid leans into the obvious call harder than anyone, so you can sit on the pass and be right four times in five. Now do that in every situation, down, distance and score, and add it up. Against Reid a defense calls about 3 more plays per 100 correctly than against an average team. That gap is what predictable means here.

The flip

Readable, and better for it

Raw numbers say unpredictable teams score more. That is a mirage.

Good offenses simply live in friendlier down and distance, where the run-pass split is naturally balanced. Compare each caller to the league in the exact same spots and the sign flips.

r = −0.37

More predictable, once you control for the situation, means a better offense.

Reid sits at the readable extreme and runs the best offense in football. So do McVay, LaFleur and Joe Brady. Switch the yardstick and nothing moves.

Where it pays

Surprise pays nowhere

So should a caller ever go off script to stay unpredictable? Not on this evidence.

Being predictable helps most on early downs, where committing to what works beats disguise. The edge is smaller on obvious passing downs and undetectable in the thin late-and-short samples. But no situation rewards being harder to guess. Nothing lands negative.

2025

This year's play-callers

Every team's current play-caller on the same scale.

Joe Brady and Andy Reid sit at the readable extreme and run two of the league's best offenses. Chip Kelly is the cautionary tale at the other end: hard to guess, going nowhere. And Ben Johnson, now in Chicago, remains the exception that proves the rule.

The verdict

Unpredictability is not the skill. Knowing what is coming and stopping it are very different problems, and the best play-callers bet on that difference. They find what works, commit to it, and dare eleven men to do something about it.

Being hard to read, it turns out, is mostly what struggling coordinators do while they look for something that works.
