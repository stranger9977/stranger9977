# Pacing — the density fix

Feedback from the first video scenes: **too dense, hard to keep up, not a pleasant
viewing experience.** That is the most important craft note we have, and this file is the
correction. It applies to every scene regardless of which renderer produces it.

Density is the default failure of data-driven video, because the maker already knows the
finding and forgets that the viewer is meeting it for the first time. Everything below
exists to slow you down.

## The five rules

1. **One new idea per beat.** If the viewer has to track two changes at once, they track
   neither.
2. **Never animate and explain simultaneously.** Move the thing, *stop*, then say what it
   means. Motion and narration compete for the same attention.
3. **Every beat ends in stillness — 1.5 to 2.8 seconds.** This feels agonising while you
   are building it and correct when you watch it back. Trust the discomfort.
4. **Maximum ~8 words on screen at once.** If the caption needs a comma, it is two beats.
5. **One colour does the work.** A palette of three states — resting, emphasised,
   absent — is enough for almost any finding. A fourth colour is usually a second idea
   smuggled in.

## The stillness test

Watch it back at full speed with the sound off. If you feel rushed, it is too fast. If
you feel slightly bored, it is close to right. **Narration will fill the gaps you left,
and the gaps are what make it followable.**

## Why this is not a compromise

Slow is the format's advantage, not its limitation. The audience — a dad and his kid
settling an argument — is not scanning. They are watching to understand something well
enough to repeat it. Fern's reconstructions hold on a single shot for a long time.
Bois's charts accumulate patiently and let you see the shape coming before it is named.

Compression is what a highlight package does. **You are not making a highlight package.**

## Worked example

`../examples/embiid_availability.py` — 46 seconds, six beats, one finding.

| Beat | Idea | Hold |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 78 playoff games. Eight postseasons. | 1.8s + 2.0s |
| 02 | The 78, as a grid. | 1.6s |
| 03 | 12 he missed entirely. | 2.2s |
| 04 | 52 he played hurt. | 2.4s |
| 05 | 14 he started healthy. | 2.6s |
| 06 | All 14 in a first round. None after Game 4. | 2.6s + 2.8s + 3.0s |

Six beats in 46 seconds is roughly **one idea every 7–8 seconds.** That is the target
rate for a finding-driven scene. Note the holds get *longer* as the stakes rise — the
last beat sits for three full seconds before the rule draws under it.
