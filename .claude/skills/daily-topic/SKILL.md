# Pick the day's topic

This is the first five minutes of the production hour, and it is the highest-leverage
five minutes in the whole process. The idea sets the ceiling. Everything downstream —
the analysis, the writing, the design, the narration — can only realize the ceiling the
idea establishes. A perfectly executed video on a topic nobody is arguing about is a
perfectly executed video nobody watches.

Timebox it. Five minutes, three options, pick one, move on. Do not let topic selection
eat the hour.

## The filter

A topic qualifies only if it clears all four:

1. **It's in the conversation.** Somebody is arguing about it right now, today.
2. **You have a real opinion.** The argument-with-your-father-in-law test. If you don't
   care, the writing will be flat and no amount of process fixes that.
3. **It's testable.** There is data that could settle it, or at least meaningfully move
   it. A pure values disagreement is not a piece for this channel.
4. **The answer might surprise you.** If you already know the finding and it confirms
   the conventional wisdom, there is no reversal, and without a reversal there is no
   piece. See `channel/style/house-style.md`.

The fourth filter is the one people skip and the one that matters most.

## Where to look

In priority order, five minutes total:

- **Your own timeline.** @run_the_sims follows the right people. What are analytics
  people and beat writers actually arguing about?
- **The broadcast cliché of the week.** Somebody said something confident and unexamined
  on a national broadcast. That is the belief on trial.
- **The argument you actually had.** Your father-in-law, group chat, a coworker. These
  are reliably the best topics because you already know the steelman by heart.
- **The evergreen queue.** `channel/ideas/backlog.md`. When nothing topical clears the
  filter, pull from the bank. This is normal and expected — do not force a topical piece.

## Timing reality

Topical sports videos have a short window. If the discourse broke yesterday, you are
already late for the search spike, and by the time a 12-minute researched piece ships
you may be publishing into a dead topic.

So bias toward **beliefs, not events**. An event decays in 48 hours. A belief — "you
have to establish the run," "ice the kicker," "he can't win the big one" — is
permanently in the conversation and spikes every time an event touches it. That is what
makes the *Against the Book* format compound instead of decay: the piece stays true and
stays searchable long after the week's news is gone.

Use the news as the **hook**, not the subject. The event gets you the click this week;
the belief gets you the views for three years.

## Output

Pitch exactly three, in this format, then stop and let the creator choose:

```
### Option A — [working title, two words if possible]

**The belief:** [stated as a real person would say it, ideally a quote]
**Why now:** [what happened, who's arguing]
**The test:** [what data would settle it, and whether you have access to it]
**Possible reversal:** [the turn, if the data breaks the interesting way]
**Risk:** [what makes this hard — data doesn't exist, finding might be boring, already covered]
```

Be honest in the Risk line. A pitch that hides its weakness wastes the hour that
follows.

After the creator picks, hand off to `validate-idea` to lock the title and thumbnail
before any analysis starts.
