# League Footage Policy — Reference

Evidence behind the decision table in `SKILL.md`. Confidence is tagged throughout:
**[Documented]** = policy text or court/primary record. **[Reported]** = credible
journalism or first-hand creator account. **[Inference]** = reasoning from documented
mechanics.

Last researched July 2026. **Re-verify before betting a channel on it** — the UFC and NHL
entries in particular moved within the last eighteen months.

## The three systems

| System | Trigger | Consequence | Fair use? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content ID** | Automated fingerprint match | Monetize-claim, track, or block. **No channel penalty.** | **No** |
| **DMCA takedown** | A human deciding to act | Video removed **+ copyright strike** | No |
| **YPP inauthentic/reused content** | YouTube reviewing originality | **Channel demonetization** | N/A |

**[Documented]** Three strikes in 90 days = termination. A strike also removes the video,
blocks uploads for a week, and stays on record 90 days.
[Strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000) ·
[claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276)

### Claim mechanics

**[Documented]** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7000961))

- Rightsholder sets policy per match: **Monetize** (they take the ad revenue, video stays
  up), **Track** (invisible to you), or **Block** (unwatchable in some or all territories).
- A claim does **not** affect channel standing or move you toward termination.
- **Dispute** → claimant has **30 days**. No response = claim expires, released.
- Claimant rejects → **appeal** → claimant has **7 days**. If they escalate, they must file
  a DMCA takedown, which converts a money problem into a strike problem.
- Revenue during a dispute is held and paid to whoever prevails.

## League table

### Tier 1 — Avoid footage entirely

**NCAA / College football — highest risk, and the enforcer is not who you'd guess.**

**[Reported, well-sourced]** The threat is **XOS Digital, a division of Catapult Sports**,
holding 100+ collegiate rights partnerships and claiming the largest college sports library
in the world. In May 2025 **Brett Kollmann** — a heavily transformative film-breakdown
creator, *and himself an NFL Access Pass participant* — uploaded draft analysis on May 1,
got a claim May 5 citing a raw coaching-film file
(`UTSA 091424 Texas OFF vs TEX UTSA O.mp4`), with notice the video would be **removed with
a copyright strike on May 12**. Reporting notes Catapult usually wins because independent
creators won't fight an entity that size. Outcome unreported.
[Wide Left](https://www.wideleft.football/p/how-catapult-sports-is-killing-football) ·
[Sportsnaut](https://sportsnaut.com/college-football/college-football-ncaa-investigation-film/)

**[Documented]** Rights are also fragmented — conference networks (ESPN owns ACC/SEC, FOX
owns Big Ten), individual schools, and NCAA championship footage licensed through
[Veritone](https://licensing.veritone.com/). There is no single door to knock on.
[NCAA footage policy](https://www.ncaa.com/_flysystem/public-s3/files/Footage%20Usage%20Licensing_1.pdf)

> **Rule: never use college coaching film, All-22, or school-sourced video.** It is the
> only category with a documented strike outcome rather than a claim. Broadcast college
> footage is safer than coaching film but still multi-party.
>
> **This directly constrains the NIL / transfer-portal lane.** Build it on data
> visualization, licensed stills, diagrams and original interviews — not on film.

**Soccer — Premier League / UEFA / FIFA.** **[Documented + Reported]** Rapid fingerprint
takedowns. The PL struck the **KSI/Sidemen Charity Match 2023** livestream. A creator
publicly reported **four strikes from UEFA and the Premier League** in October 2025 —
the only documented multi-strike-on-one-creator case in the research. FIFA enforces via
monitoring, platform cooperation and court orders; the YouTube–FIFA World Cup 2026
partnership grants ordinary creators **nothing**.
[Justia](https://answers.justia.com/question/2025/10/16/received-4-copyright-strikes-from-uefa-a-1079682)

**UFC.** **[Documented]** EFF documented UFC sending **five DMCA takedowns** to John
MacKay's "Boxing Now" commentary channel — over videos using **still images**. He
counter-noticed each time and YouTube reinstated, because UFC never sued.
[EFF](https://www.eff.org/press/releases/youtube-user-fights-unfair-takedown-campaign-ufc)
**[Reported, thin]** UFC moved to **Paramount+** in 2026 ($7.7B, 7 years); creator reports
say Paramount claims more aggressively than ESPN, down to 15-second edited clips. Single
source — monitor, don't treat as established.

**NHL — got worse recently.** **[Reported, strong]** January 2025 the NHL worked through
creator back catalogs *and* new uploads, **manually claiming** and taking all revenue.
Named: Hockey Psychology, Next Man Up; livestream blocks against 2BC Productions.
EckhartsLadder publicly doubted NHL content was viable going forward.
[Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2025/01/16/the-nhl-just-cant-get-social-media-right/) ·
[Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/nhl/copyright-claim-glitch-unveils-uneasy-relationship.html)

**F1 — aggressive and inaccurate.** **[Documented + Reported]** FOM's system in 2025 was
mass-claiming **F1 25 video game footage**, falsely matching it to real race footage.
FOM also sent cease-and-desists in August 2024 over creators merely using "F1" in channel
branding. International feed footage is exclusively licensed to broadcast partners — even
drivers are limited. No F1-wide creator license exists.
[F1 guidelines](https://www.formula1.com/en/information/guidelines.4EOKE9RRqevL4niTK9kWyt)

### Tier 2 — Aggressive claims, low strike risk, real licensed door

**NFL.** **[Documented]** The league owns the copyright in its broadcasts (rights assigned
by teams), so there's one well-organized owner. Its "no use without express written
consent" boilerplate is legally overbroad — the *Nebraska Law Review* wrote a piece on it —
but it accurately describes how the NFL behaves. Unlicensed NFL footage in commentary
reliably draws claims.
[Conduct Detrimental](https://www.conductdetrimental.com/post/who-owns-the-intellectual-property-rights-to-sports-broadcasts) ·
[Nebraska Law Review](https://lawreview.unl.edu/warning-nfl-claims-copyright-ownership-everything/)

**MLB.** Was aggressive, is pivoting for partners only. See the Jomboy section.

### Tier 3 — Genuinely more permissive

**NBA — the most creator-friendly major league.** **[Documented]** Adam Silver: *"The games
are the meals and the highlights are the snacks. And we encourage our fans to snack before
meals."* The league consciously let basketball social media, including amateur in-game
highlights, run.
[SBD](https://sportsbusinessdaily.com/Daily/Issues/2015/11/20/Leagues-and-Governing-Bodies/NBA.aspx) ·
[CNBC Feb 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/19/nba-embraces-content-creators-tries-to-protect-live-sports-rights.html)

**[Inference]** Permissive posture ≠ absent Content ID. The NBA does maintain references.
The permissiveness shows up as tolerance and a licensing pipeline, not as an absence of
claims. No evidence found of the NBA mass-claiming analysis channels — weak evidence of
tolerance.

## Jomboy — the correction

**[Documented]** The story creators tell each other ("Jomboy proved frame-by-frame is fair
use") is wrong, and it matters.

- MLB sent **annual mass copyright violation notices** and **claimed the revenue** on his
  videos, including the lip-reading breakdowns. O'Brien described it publicly in May 2024.
  Reporting is explicit: **MLB won even when fair use was claimed.**
- He built the business anyway — podcasts, merch, live events — while MLB took the YouTube
  ad money. He absorbed claims as the cost of audience-building.
- June 2025: MLB acquired an **undisclosed minority stake**. O'Brien: *"We got no money.
  They bought out other investors."* A source said the deal **completely clears Jomboy's
  channel** to use MLB footage, plus vault access and league IP for merch.
- It took **four years** to negotiate.

[Variety](https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/mlb-acquires-stake-jomboy-media-jimmy-obrien-content-deal-1236424282/) ·
[Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/mlb-jomboy-stake-creators-youtube/) ·
[Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2025/11/21/jimmy-obrien-jomboy-media-partnership-with-mlb-took-four-years-to-come-together/)

**It's not a legal template, it's an acquisition outcome.** He was never protected.

**[Documented]** Related: **Rob Friedman (@PitchingNinja)** had his Twitter account
suspended by an MLB DMCA complaint despite single-pitch clips with commentary. Resolved by
**negotiated agreement**, not a fair use ruling. Same pattern — relationship beat doctrine.

**[High confidence]** No evidence the Jomboy deal changed MLB's posture toward anyone else.
Assume 2019 behavior, when Techdirt documented MLB claiming revenue on obviously-fair-use
videos.
[Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2019/08/22/why-is-mlb-claiming-revenue-obviously-fair-use-videos-youtube/)

## Folklore vs. reality

### Myths

| Belief | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Under 7 seconds is safe" / "10 seconds" | **Traceable myth.** The figure circulating in sports-creator communities comes from SEO/AI-generated guides, not YouTube and not any league. No published duration safe harbor exists; partners configure their own thresholds. |
| Zoom / crop / mirror / flip | Fingerprinting is robust to all of it. |
| Speed change (95%, 103%) | Same. Audio ID explicitly survives pitch-shift. |
| Telestration, arrows, screen-in-screen | **Nothing** for detection. Do them for fair use and YPP originality, not to dodge Content ID. |
| "Transformative use prevents claims" | Content ID **cannot evaluate fair use.** Transformation changes your legal position, not your detection probability. [EFF](https://www.eff.org/wp/unfiltered-how-youtubes-content-id-discourages-fair-use-and-dictates-what-we-see-online) |

### Real

| Practice | Why | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Mute broadcast audio** | Kills the **music** claim — arena music, walk-up songs and broadcast beds carry *separate* music-industry references. Does **not** stop the league's audiovisual reference. | **[Inference, high]** |
| **Unlisted test upload** | Surfaces matches before launch while you can still re-cut. Free. | **[Documented]** |
| **Licensed footage** | The only thing that actually works. | **[Documented]** |
| **Original commentary + editorial structure** | Protects **channel-level** monetization under YPP. YouTube confirmed in the July 2025 rename that commentary and clips remain fine **if you add significant original value**. | **[Documented]** ([policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)) |
| **Own camera, licensed stills, data viz, animation, diagrams** | No reference file to match. Why the diagram/animation style is structurally dominant for explainers. | **[Documented]** |

## Licensing paths

**NFL Access Pass** — the strongest program for an explainer channel, and the one to chase.
Launched 2023. Pre-approved NFL-owned clips, event access (Super Bowl, Draft, Combine), a
direct NFL business contact, and **the right to monetize videos using that footage without
being claimed.** 24 creators in year one, 200M+ cumulative views. Participants include
Brett Kollmann, Tom Grossi, Peighton Tubre. **Access Pass for Legends** (Jan 2025) extends
it to former players. **No public application form or eligibility bar found** — appears
invite/curated via NFL + YouTube. No fee documented; it's a marketing program, not a paid
license. **[Confidence: medium-high]**
[Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/the-nfl-gives-creators-access-to-its-archives-for-content-creation-with-pre-approved-footage/) ·
[NFL Media](https://media.nfl.com/news-and-releases/sponsorship/-nfl-and-youtube-debut--access-pass-for-legends---opening-doors-)

**NBA Creator Program / WSC Sports** — **[Documented]** select creators get **25,000 hours**
of game footage (2014–2024) plus 2,500 hours current-season, with AI editing tools, and can
license highlights for original videos. For scale: from 2016 the NBA granted **five minutes
per game** (~50 hrs/season). Curated; terms undisclosed.
[Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/why-the-nba-is-helping-basketball-creators-make-longer-youtube-videos/) ·
[NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/nba-creator-program-expands-2024-25)

**Stock and editorial**
- **Getty editorial video** — rights-managed only, no cheap subscription tier. Roughly
  **$99 for limited distribution up to thousands** for broad use; low-res ~$130/download,
  4K ~$300/download minimum in prepaid packs. Indicative, not quoted.
  [Getty](https://www.gettyimages.com/plans-and-pricing)
- **Imagn** — sports **stills**, good and cheap. Not a footage solution.
  [imagn.com](https://imagn.com/)
- **Veritone** — the path for NCAA championship footage.
- **IMG Video Archive** — 500k+ hours, quote-based.

**Reality check:** at Getty editorial rates a 12-minute explainer using 15 clips is
plausibly **$1,500–4,500 in licensing.** Which is why almost nobody does it, and why league
programs are the only economically viable licensed route for a solo creator.

## Fair use, as it actually lands

1. **Purpose/character** — genuinely favorable. Criticism and comment are enumerated;
   tactical analysis is about as transformative as it gets.
2. **Nature of the work** — mildly favorable. Live sports are factual events, though the
   broadcast (camera choices, replays, graphics) is creative expression. Contested.
3. **Amount** — favorable if you take only what the point requires. This is where clip
   discipline matters — for defensibility, not detection.
4. **Market effect** — **your weakest factor.** Leagues license official highlight
   products; a court could find a clip-heavy video substitutes for one.
   [USC ILJ](https://gould.usc.edu/why/students/orgs/ilj/assets/docs/17-1%20Mellow.pdf)

**[Documented]** Nearest litigated analog: *Hosseinzadeh v. Klein* (S.D.N.Y., Aug 2017) —
a reaction/commentary video held fair use, short segments interspersed with substantial
critical commentary. But the court **expressly refused to hold reaction videos categorically
fair use**.
[Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/hosseinzadeh-klein-sdny2017.pdf)

## Documented outcomes

| Case | Outcome | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Rob Moody / Robelinda2** (cricket, 1M+ subs) | **Channel terminated** Nov 2023 after a series of strikes | **[Documented]** |
| **KSI / Sidemen Charity Match 2023** | Livestream removed, PL strike | **[Documented]** |
| **John MacKay / Boxing Now vs UFC** | **Successful defense.** 5 takedowns, 5 counter-notices, reinstated each time when UFC declined to sue. Lost days of revenue each round. | **[Documented]** |
| **Brett Kollmann vs XOS/Catapult** | Claim + threatened strike, May 2025. Outcome unreported. | **[Reported]** |
| **NHL creators** | Revenue redirected or blocked; no terminations reported | **[Reported]** |
| **Jomboy Media** | Years of claims → MLB equity stake + clearance | **[Documented]** |
| **PitchingNinja** | Suspended by MLB DMCA → negotiated resolution | **[Documented]** |

**The pattern in every successful defense:** counter-notice and outlast (claimant won't
sue), or negotiate (you're big enough to talk to). **Nobody in this record won on fair use
in court.**

## Gaps

- **NFL Access Pass eligibility** — no public criteria. Worth a direct inquiry to NFL
  business development.
- **Paramount/UFC 2026 severity** — single creator post. Monitor.
- **A "FIFA Digital Archive" open to creators** — asserted only by AI-generated content
  farms. **Do not rely on it.**
- **Current MLB claim rate against non-partner channels** — not measurable. Assume 2019
  behavior.
- **Content ID match-length threshold** — YouTube publishes none and partners configure
  it. This is precisely why no duration is "safe."
